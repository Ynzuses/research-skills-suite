# Panel Design

## The Diversity Ladder

Reviewers add information only to the extent that their errors are unrelated. Build the panel from the highest rung the host and the privacy rule allow, and say in the closing report which rung was reached.

| Rung | What differs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Model family | Different training and different habitual errors. Needs an eligible route to another provider. |
| 2 | Materials | One reviewer reads the artifact with its sources, another the artifact alone, another only the methods and outputs. |
| 3 | Lens | Each reviewer is responsible for one way the artifact could be wrong. |
| 4 | Reading order or role | Conclusions first versus methods first; reader versus opponent. The weakest difference. |

Rungs combine. A panel in which every reviewer shares the model, the materials, and the brief is one reviewer run several times; its agreement is repetition.

## Lenses by Artifact Type

Pick three or four. The opposing reviewer is listed last in each row and should be kept.

| Artifact | Lenses |
| --- | --- |
| Manuscript or report | argument and reader order; methods and statistics; support for each claim in the cited source or result; the strongest case for rejection |
| Literature review | coverage and missed terms; claim-to-source support; balance, including contrary evidence; whether the synthesis follows from the evidence |
| Analysis plan or study design | whether the design can answer the question; leakage, confounding, and unit of analysis; feasibility and failure handling; what result would be uninterpretable |
| Statistical or method decision | assumptions against the data; alternatives that were not considered; sensitivity to defaults and thresholds; the case for the simplest alternative |
| Scientific code and results | correctness against the stated method; provenance and silent failures (missing versus zero, truncated inputs); whether figures and tables can be regenerated from the outputs; an attempt to produce a wrong result that still passes |
| Figure or table | whether each number traces to an authoritative output; whether the encoding misleads; consistency with the text; what a hostile reader would conclude |

A domain lens (the organism, instrument, or field convention) replaces one of these when domain facts carry the argument.

## Panel Size

- **One independent reviewer:** moderate stakes, or a narrow question with one way to be wrong.
- **Three or four:** the default for an artifact about to be circulated or a decision that is costly to reverse.
- **More than five:** rarely useful. Coordination and adjudication grow faster than new findings. Split the artifact instead.

## What a Reviewer Receives

Every reviewer receives the same frame:

- the artifact and its identity;
- its purpose and intended reader;
- the claims or decisions at stake;
- the authorities it may open, and any it must not;
- what is out of scope.

Each reviewer also receives its own lens and nothing about the other reviewers: not their lenses' findings, not the author's expectations of what will be found, and not earlier review rounds unless the task is to check a repair.

Do not ask for a score or an overall verdict. Scores invite averaging, and a reviewer that must grade tends to soften findings to fit the grade.

## What a Reviewer Returns

For each finding:

- a short statement of what is wrong or unsupported;
- the location in the artifact;
- the evidence opened to reach it, with its location, or a statement that no evidence was opened;
- a severity;
- what would resolve it.

And once per review: what the reviewer could not check and why.

## Severity

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Blocking | The artifact's main claim, result, or decision would be wrong or unsupported if this stands. |
| Major | A reader would be misled on a material point, or a required element is missing. |
| Minor | A real defect that does not change what a reader concludes. |
| Question | The reviewer could not locate the problem or the evidence. It needs an answer before it can be a finding. |

Severity is the reviewer's proposal. The adjudicator may change it after opening the evidence.
